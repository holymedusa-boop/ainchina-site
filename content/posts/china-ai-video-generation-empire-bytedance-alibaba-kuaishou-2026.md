## The $15 Million-a-Day Lesson

On March 25, 2026, OpenAI did something it had never done before: it shut down a headline product. **Sora**, the text-to-video model that had ignited the generative video race in February 2024, was discontinued. The API was scheduled to sunset in September. The reason was not regulatory pressure, not a technical failure, and not a strategic pivot toward some newer architecture. It was simple arithmetic. Sora was burning roughly **$15 million per day** in compute costs while generating approximately **$2.1 million in total lifetime revenue**. At roughly $1.30 per 10-second clip in raw generation cost, the unit economics never closed.

The shutdown was unprecedented. No frontier lab had ever fully retreated from a modality it had pioneered. Sora had defined the category, set public expectations, and forced every major AI lab to announce a video generation program. And then it died—not from competition, but from its own balance sheet.

What happened next reshaped the global AI video market faster than anyone predicted. By August 2026, **eight of the top ten models** on the Artificial Analysis Video Arena leaderboard were Chinese. ByteDance's **Seedance 2.0** was generating more than **1 billion RMB per month** in revenue. Kuaishou's **Kling AI** had raised **$3 billion** at an **$18 billion valuation**. Alibaba's **HappyHorse 1.1** had claimed the #1 spot on multiple global benchmarks. The AI video generation market, projected to reach **$3.67 billion by 2028**, had become a Chinese domain.

## ByteDance Seedance: The Revenue King

Of the three dominant Chinese platforms, **ByteDance's Seedance 2.0** is the most commercially successful. Released in February 2026, it quickly established itself as the default choice for high-volume video generation, particularly in short-form content and advertising where ByteDance's ecosystem—Douyin, Jianying, and Volcano Engine—creates an almost unbeatable distribution advantage.

The financial numbers are staggering. As of the first half of 2026, Seedance was generating **more than 1 billion RMB per month** in revenue, translating to an annualized recurring revenue (ARR) of approximately **$2 billion**. The business operates at an estimated **70% gross margin**. Seedance holds approximately **95% penetration in China's short drama industry** and commands **over 80% of daily token consumption volume** in the AI video generation market.

The technical capabilities that underpin this commercial dominance are equally impressive. Seedance 2.0 accepts four input types simultaneously—text, images, videos, and audio—delivering complete audiovisual output in a single API call. It supports resolutions up to 4K, durations up to 60 seconds at 2K, and features sub-120ms lip-sync latency across eight languages. The model's Elo rating on the Artificial Analysis Video Arena reached **1,269** as of mid-2026.

But Seedance's dominance has also created backlash. In mid-2026, ByteDance **indefinitely shelved the international rollout** of Seedance 2.0 following copyright complaints from major Hollywood studios. The freeze left enterprise buyers in North America and Europe scrambling for alternatives, a gap that Alibaba's HappyHorse and Kuaishou's Kling have aggressively moved to fill.

## Kling AI: The $18 Billion Spin-Off

If Seedance represents ecosystem integration, **Kling AI** represents platform independence. Originally incubated inside Kuaishou, Kling emerged as the most credible independent challenger to ByteDance's dominance. By early 2026, the business had grown large enough that Kuaishou made the decision to spin it out as a standalone entity.

The spin-off was accompanied by one of the largest funding rounds in Chinese AI history. In July 2026, Kling AI announced a **$3 billion Series A** with a post-money valuation of approximately **$18 billion**. The investor list featured a rare simultaneous appearance of **Tencent, Alibaba Cloud, and Baidu**—alongside 34 institutions led by CPE Yuanfeng and Guofang Innovation. The valuation approached roughly 70% of Kuaishou's own market capitalization.

Kling's revenue is driven by two pillars: API calls from B2B enterprise clients and paid consumer subscriptions. The company has found particular traction in professional content production, providing virtual scene generation and special effects for Chinese television dramas and Hollywood productions. The 3.0 series, launched in February 2026, enables up to 15-second video generation, storyboard control, subject consistency, and synchronized audio-video output.

The funding round included a critical performance clause: if Kling fails to complete a qualified IPO before October 30, 2031, investors can demand repurchases at principal plus **8% annual simple interest**—approaching **$4.3 billion** at full funding. Multiple media sources report Kuaishou plans to initiate Kling's Hong Kong IPO within 12 months, which would make it one of the largest Chinese tech IPOs since 2021.

## Alibaba HappyHorse: The Benchmark Champion

While Seedance dominated by ecosystem and Kling by scale, **Alibaba's HappyHorse** took a different path: it won on pure quality. Released anonymously in April 2026 on the Artificial Analysis Video Arena, HappyHorse 1.0 quickly climbed to the **#1 position** in both text-to-video and image-to-video categories—a feat achieved through blind user evaluations where raters did not know which model produced which clip. The model scored **1,444 Elo points**, surpassing Google's Veo 3.1 and xAI's Grok Imagine Video.

**HappyHorse 1.1**, released June 22, 2026, solidified this position. The upgrade delivered improvements across five dimensions: motion expressiveness, subject consistency, instruction following, visual quality, and audio synchronization. Key additions included 9-image reference-to-video for multi-character consistency and emotional audio with built-in lip-sync across seven languages.

Alibaba's strategy with HappyHorse is explicitly **API-first and enterprise-focused**. The model is available through Alibaba Cloud Model Studio with full enterprise SLAs, security certifications, and regional compliance. A 40% launch discount brought production-quality AI video within reach of mid-market companies that previously considered the technology too expensive for experimentation.

Perhaps most significantly, Alibaba has committed to **open-sourcing HappyHorse**. The 15-billion-parameter model weights and public API were planned for release in April 2026, making HappyHorse one of the largest open-source video generation models available. This strategy mirrors Alibaba's broader approach with the Qwen language model series, where open-sourcing has built developer loyalty and ecosystem momentum.

## The Pricing War: From Premium Commodity to Mass Market

One of the most consequential developments in AI video generation during 2026 has been the collapse of pricing. What began as a premium technology accessible only to well-funded studios has become a commodity service priced by the second.

The pricing dynamics reveal the strategic differentiators of each platform. Kling 3.0 has pursued aggressive price-performance, offering 720p generation at roughly **$0.10 per second**—the lowest price among major platforms. Seedance 2.0 offers a tiered model: the Mini variant at **$0.15 per second** for 720p, and the Pro variant at **$0.30 per second** for 4K output. HappyHorse 1.1 sits in the middle at **$0.14 per second** for 720p, with the added value of native synchronized audio included at no extra cost.

Volcano Engine raised its 2026 MaaS revenue target from 1.5 billion RMB to **15 billion RMB**, a 10x increase driven almost entirely by Seedance.

Between July 3 and July 23, **five companies** announced large-scale financing deals totaling more than **26.2 billion RMB**—surpassing the total funding the entire AI video track attracted over the previous two years (2024–2025).

The concentration of capital was not coincidental. It reflected two converging forces. First, **ByteDance's Seedance created a siphon effect**. With monthly revenue exceeding 1 billion RMB and a 70% gross margin, Seedance demonstrated that AI video could be extraordinarily profitable.

Second, the **commercialization of AI video had been validated across every major use case**—advertising, e-commerce, short dramas, gaming, and film. Kling AI's ARR approaching $500 million, Shengshu Technology's 10x revenue growth in 2025, and Aishi Technology's $40 million ARR provided the "visible cash flow" that venture capital requires for billion-dollar deployments.

The flood of capital is already reshaping competitive dynamics. Companies that raised in July 2026 are using the funds to stockpile compute and accelerate model iteration. Those that did not raise face a narrowing window: as ByteDance executes a dual-pronged attack of model capability plus price warfare, competitors that cannot match its R&D spending risk being priced out entirely.

## What Comes Next: IPOs, Consolidation, and the Next Battle

The AI video generation market in 2026 is simultaneously mature and nascent. The technology works. The revenue is real. But three forces will shape the next eighteen months.

**First, the IPO wave.** Kling AI's planned Hong Kong IPO within 12 months will be a bellwether. If Kling sustains its $18 billion valuation in public markets, it validates AI video as a standalone asset class. ByteDance, which remains private, may face renewed pressure to list Seedance separately.

**Second, international expansion.** The Seedance freeze demonstrated that Chinese AI video platforms face significant regulatory and legal barriers in Western markets. The companies that can navigate these barriers—Kling AI, with its 70% overseas revenue share, has shown the most progress—will capture the global market. Those that cannot will be confined to China and Belt-and-Road countries.

**Third, the integration of video into general AI agents.** Video generation is increasingly not a standalone product but a capability within larger AI platforms. ByteDance is integrating Seedance into Doubao. Alibaba is embedding HappyHorse into its Qwen app and enterprise tools. The standalone video generation startup may become as rare as the standalone image generation startup already is.

|---|---|---|

| **H1 2027** | Kling AI Hong Kong IPO | Validates AI video sector valuation |
| **2027** | International expansion by Chinese platforms | Regulatory battles in US and EU markets |
| **2027–2028** | Market consolidation | M&A of smaller players by ByteDance, Alibaba, Kuaishou |
| **2028** | Market size projected at $3.67B | 23.6% CAGR from 2025 |
| **2028+** | Video as agent capability | Integration into general-purpose AI platforms |

*Table 7: Projected timeline for AI video generation market evolution. The next 18 months will determine whether Chinese platforms can convert domestic dominance into global market leadership. Data: Industry analyst projections, company guidance, regulatory filings.*

The broader implications extend beyond video generation itself. The Chinese AI video sector's trajectory—from fast follower to global leader in under three years—demonstrates a pattern repeating across AI modalities. In language models, Chinese labs now match or exceed Western performance. In chip design, domestic accelerators approach NVIDIA parity. Video generation is simply the latest category where the assumption of American technological leadership no longer holds.

What the Sora collapse and the Seedance rise demonstrate is that in AI, unit economics and ecosystem integration matter as much as technical capability. OpenAI built a better model but could not build a better business. ByteDance built a business so tightly integrated into its ecosystem that competitors struggle to match its distribution even when they match its quality. The lesson: the winners will not be the labs with the most parameters or highest benchmark scores, but the companies that convert technical capability into sustainable, profitable, scaled products.

China's AI video generation empire is already built. The question now is how far it extends beyond China's borders—and who can afford to compete.
