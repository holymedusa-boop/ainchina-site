## The Bunny Nobody Saw Coming

There was no launch event. No blog post. No benchmark card. No press outreach, no embargoed briefings, no founder thread on X.

On September 23, 2026, a model listed as **Space Bunny Alpha** quietly appeared on OpenRouter — the model-routing platform that has become the de facto scoreboard for the global AI industry — and on OpenCode, a developer-focused coding platform. The listing said it was a Flash-class model optimized for fast reasoning and programming. It offered a one-million-token context window, accepted text, image, and video inputs, supported adjustable reasoning intensity, and provided tool-calling capability.

The price was $0 per million tokens. Inbound and outbound. Free.

Nobody knew who built it. The developer field was blank.

Four days later, over the Mid-Autumn Festival holiday in China, Space Bunny did something no anonymous model had ever done: it topped the **daily token-consumption rankings on both OpenRouter and OpenCode simultaneously**, and stayed there for three consecutive days. On September 27 alone, developers ran approximately **3.99 trillion tokens through Space Bunny on OpenRouter** and roughly **8.3 trillion on OpenCode** — more than any named model from OpenAI, Google, Anthropic, or any Chinese lab with an actual logo on the door.

By the end of its first week, Space Bunny Alpha had processed **13.9 trillion tokens**, placing it **third in the world** on OpenRouter's weekly global rankings — ahead of every model except DeepSeek's V4.1 Flash and Zhipu's GLM 5.3 Flash. Two of the three most-used AI models on Earth were now Chinese. The third had no name at all.

The AI developer internet did what it always does when confronted with a mystery: it started investigating. What followed was a week of crowdsourced forensic analysis — tokenizer fingerprinting, behavioral probing, contradiction cataloging — that pointed to one increasingly obvious suspect. On September 27, the same day Space Bunny hit its peak, **MiniMax** released M3.1-Flash-Preview, a coding model with strikingly similar specifications, inside its own MiniMax Code product.

MiniMax has not confirmed anything. As of this writing, the bunny's official identity remains unverified.

But the pattern is unmistakable — and it says something important about where the AI race is heading.

## The Spec Sheet

Space Bunny Alpha's published specifications, as listed on OpenRouter, describe a model built for exactly one job: fast, reliable, long-context software development.

The spec sheet reads like a direct answer to what developers have been asking for all year: a model fast enough to live inside an IDE, smart enough to handle real repository-scale work, and cheap enough to use for routine tasks without thinking about the meter. The one-million-token context means an entire large codebase fits in a single session. The adjustable reasoning ladder means a developer can spend milliseconds on boilerplate and minutes on architecture.

And the price — zero — removed the last reason not to try it.

What developers did with it, within days, ranged from impressive to showy. They built web games, music players, and backend APIs. One X creator named siddharth gave Space Bunny a text prompt and a handful of images and asked for a seaside-themed game; the model produced boats, stations, and a coherent color palette, and the demo video passed 80,000 views. Another developer reportedly generated a detailed 3D model from reference images in about an hour. A third built a working invoicing application from photographs of paper receipts in four minutes.

The free window was listed as one week. That was enough.

## The Detective Work

An anonymous model at the top of the global leaderboard is, in the AI community, an irresistible provocation. Within hours of Space Bunny's appearance, developers across Reddit, X, and Chinese forums began the collective work of unmasking it.

The first and most obvious technique was the one everyone uses: ask the model who it is. The results were a study in contradiction.

Some users reported Space Bunny identifying itself in ways that hinted at Moonshot AI's Kimi line — the natural first guess, since Moonshot had been telegraphing a next-generation model. Others got responses that referenced OpenAI, which complicated the picture. A few got answers that mentioned nothing at all, just the kind of careful deflection that suggests either a well-tuned system prompt or genuine uncertainty baked into the model's identity layer.

Direct interrogation was a dead end. So the community turned to forensics.

The decisive technique was **tokenizer fingerprinting**. Every large language model breaks text into tokens using a proprietary tokenizer, and each tokenizer produces slightly different token counts for the same strings — a signature as distinctive as a fingerprint. A third-party test ran 50 test strings through Space Bunny Alpha's tokenizer and compared the output against every major model family.

The result: **Space Bunny Alpha's token counts matched MiniMax's family exactly, across all 50 strings.**

| Fingerprinting Method | Result |
|---|---|
| Direct identity queries | Contradictory (Kimi hints, OpenAI references, deflections) |
| Tokenizer test (50 strings) | Exact match with MiniMax family |
| Reddit community consensus | Likely MiniMax M3.1 Flash preview build |
| Official confirmation | None as of September 28, 2026 |

A perfect 50-for-50 match does not happen by coincidence. Tokenizers are trained on a model's specific training data and vocabulary decisions; two models share a tokenizer fingerprint only when they come from the same lineage. The community's working conclusion: Space Bunny Alpha is a stealth preview of MiniMax's next Flash model, released anonymously to gather real-world feedback at massive scale without the reputational risk of a formal launch.

Reddit developers went further, speculating that Space Bunny was specifically the preview build of what became M3.1-Flash — a theory that gained credibility when MiniMax shipped that exact model four days later.

MiniMax, for its part, has maintained complete silence on the question. The bunny remains officially anonymous.

## The Suspect

Whether or not MiniMax ever confirms it, the company's behavior during the same week tells its own story.

On September 27 — the day Space Bunny hit its three-day, double-platform winning streak — MiniMax quietly added **M3.1-Flash-Preview** to the model picker inside MiniMax Code, its AI coding assistant. No announcement came first. Developers noticed the new option in the product before the company said anything. When MiniMax finally acknowledged it, the confirmation came through a promotional post on X, not a formal launch.

The contrast with MiniMax's own history is striking. In June 2026, the company launched M3 with full architecture details, benchmark scores, and a pricing sheet. This time: no model card, no price, no benchmark, no public API identifier. The only way to use M3.1-Flash-Preview is inside MiniMax Code.

|---|---|---|---|
| Release | Sept 27, 2026 (quiet) | June 2026 (full launch) | Earlier 2026 |
| Context window | 1M tokens | Up to 1M | — |

The specifications line up almost perfectly with Space Bunny Alpha: one-million-token context, multimodal input, adjustable reasoning up to a "max" tier, coding-first positioning. The only differences are cosmetic — M3.1-Flash-Preview currently outputs text only, while Space Bunny's listing mentions the same modality set.

MiniMax accompanied the release with a promotional campaign: from September 28 to October 7, users who check in daily on MiniMax Code receive double free credits, usable across M3.1-Flash-Preview and the company's H3 video-generation models. Token Plan quotas were reset for all users at launch.

One detail from the same week underlines how strange this strategy is: on September 28, MiniMax's stock — it trades in Hong Kong under ticker 00100 — dropped **10.1% to HK$247.4**, even as its suspected model was dominating the global conversation. The market, apparently, has not yet figured out how to price a company that wins the leaderboard anonymously.

## The Playbook

Space Bunny is not the first time a Chinese model has appeared out of nowhere and let the internet do the marketing. It is, however, the most successful execution of a pattern that has become almost standard in China this year.

| Anonymous Model | Platform | Date | Later Revealed As |
|---|---|---|---|
| Union | OpenRouter | Mid-September 2026 | Never officially confirmed |
| GLM-5.3-Flash | OpenRouter | Earlier 2026 | Zhipu AI |
| **Space Bunny Alpha** | OpenRouter, OpenCode | Sept 23, 2026 | Suspected MiniMax; unconfirmed |

The pattern is consistent: release anonymously, price at zero, let developers discover it, let the community build the detective narrative, then confirm — or quietly launch the named version — once the model has already proven itself in public.

The logic is not mysterious. A formal launch invites formal scrutiny: benchmark tables that can be compared against rivals, price points that can be undercut, capability claims that can be challenged. An anonymous release invites none of that. It generates curiosity, word-of-mouth, and — critically — real-world usage data at a scale no internal testing program could match. By the time the named version ships, the company already knows what breaks, what developers actually want, and what the model is worth.

There is also a subtler benefit: plausible deniability. If the anonymous model flops, no brand is attached. If it underperforms on a specific benchmark, there is no benchmark to point to. If it behaves badly in public, the company can disavow it — as MiniMax still technically can with Space Bunny.

In China's hyper-competitive model market — where a new flagship seems to ship every week and price wars have compressed API margins to near zero — the anonymous launch has become a hedge against the brutal economics of visibility.

## The Token War Behind the Bunny

Space Bunny's emergence is not an isolated event. It is the sharpest signal yet of a structural shift in how AI models compete.

For most of 2026, the global AI race has been measured less by benchmark scores and more by **token throughput** — the sheer volume of compute processed through a model in real-world use. It is a harder metric to game than curated evaluations, and it captures something benchmarks cannot: what developers actually choose to use, at scale, when money and time are on the line.

By that metric, China's position has become overwhelming. Chinese models have led global weekly token consumption for 22 straight weeks. The week Space Bunny entered the charts, Chinese models processed 62.22 trillion tokens — more than four times the American total — even as the overall market grew 13.18% week-over-week.

The composition of the top five tells its own story. DeepSeek's V4.1 Flash, a free open-weights model released September 10, holds the top spot with 19.6 trillion tokens and is still growing 24% week-over-week. Zhipu's GLM 5.3 Flash sits second at 16.3 trillion. Space Bunny — anonymous, unpriced, and unsponsored — outsold Tencent's Hy4 Preview nearly one-and-a-half to one. Xiaomi's newly open-sourced MiMo-V2.6-Flash entered the chart at number eight in its first week.

Notably absent from the very top: any American closed-weight model. The frontier closed models — Anthropic's Claude Fable 5.1, OpenAI's GPT-6 Astra — still win on capability benchmarks. But on the scoreboard that measures what the world actually runs at scale, the leaderboard has a distinctly Chinese accent.

Xiaomi's MiMo-V2.6 series, released open-source on September 22, adds another dimension. MiMo-V2.6-Pro, with 1.02 trillion total parameters and 42 billion active, scored 46 on the Artificial Analysis Intelligence Index — the highest of any open-source model, ahead of Moonshot's Kimi K3 and Alibaba's Qwen3.8 Max — at a price one-twentieth to one-sixtieth of overseas models at comparable intelligence levels.

The bunny did not appear in a vacuum. It appeared in the middle of an open-source, low-price, high-throughput ecosystem that has been methodically dismantling the premium-model business model for months.

## The Last Word

Space Bunny Alpha will not stay anonymous forever. Either MiniMax confirms what the tokenizer fingerprints already suggest, or the free window closes and the model quietly retires, its data-collection mission complete.

But the bunny's brief, brilliant run already taught the industry something worth remembering. In 2026, the most powerful launch strategy in AI is not a keynote, a benchmark, or a billion-dollar marketing budget. It is a free API endpoint, a catchy name, and the confidence to let the world's developers discover the product on their own.

China's labs have learned this lesson faster than anyone. They are operating in the world's most brutally competitive model market — dozens of capable labs, razor-thin margins, a developer population that is price-sensitive, technically sophisticated, and quick to switch. In that environment, stealth is not a gimmick. It is risk management, market research, and marketing rolled into one.

The deeper signal is the one hiding in the weekly token tables. For 22 consecutive weeks, Chinese models have processed more tokens than any other country's — and the gap is not narrowing. Space Bunny did not just top a leaderboard; it demonstrated that China's AI ecosystem can produce a globally competitive model, at global scale, with no brand at all.

When your anonymous side project can out-draw every named competitor on Earth, the question is no longer whether China's models can compete. It is whether the rest of the world can keep up with a playbook it hasn't figured out how to counter.

The bunny is fast, free, and — for now — officially nobody's. The industry should get used to it. There will be more.
